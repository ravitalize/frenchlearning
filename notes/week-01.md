# Week 1 (Oct 4): Greetings, calendar, alphabet, accents, pronouns

This week sets up the basics: saying hello politely, the calendar words, how French letters sound, the 5 accents and the 8 subject pronouns.

## 1. Greetings: formal (vous) or informal (tu)

> **RULE** Use the **vous** forms with strangers, teachers, shops and offices. Use the **tu** forms with friends, family and children. If you are not sure, use vous.

| Meaning | Formal (vous) | Informal (tu) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Please | S'il **vous** plaît | S'il **te** plaît |
| Excuse me (to ask something) | Excus**ez**-moi | Excus**e**-moi |
| Sorry (bumping into someone) | Pardonn**ez**-moi / Pardon | Pardonn**e**-moi / Pardon |
| What's your name? | Comment vous appelez-vous ? | Comment tu t'appelles ? / Tu t'appelles comment ? |
| How are you? | Comment allez-vous ? | Comment vas-tu ? / Ça va ? |
| Let me introduce... | Je **vous** présente... | Je **te** présente... |

> **RULE** Hello and goodbye change with the time of day. **Bonjour / Bonsoir** say hello. **Bonne journée / Bonne soirée / Bonne nuit** say goodbye.

| Hello | Goodbye | See you... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bonjour (daytime) | Bonne journée | À tout à l'heure (later today) |
| Bonsoir (evening) | Bonne soirée | À ce soir (this evening) |
| Salut ! (friends, hi or bye) | Bonne nuit (going to bed) | À demain (tomorrow) |
|  | Au revoir | À bientôt (soon) / À plus tard (later) |

Other must-know words: Merci (beaucoup), De rien, Enchanté(e), Félicitations, Bonne chance, Bienvenue, Allô (on the phone only).

Replying to "Ça va ?": Oui, ça va (bien). / Pas mal ! / Ça ne va pas.

## 2. Days, months, times of day

> **RULE** Days and months are **not capitalised** in French: lundi, novembre. Write the date as day + number + month: **samedi 14 novembre**.

- Days: lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche
- Months: janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre
- Times of day: le matin (morning), l'après-midi (afternoon), le soir (evening), la nuit (night)
- "This...": **ce** matin, **cet** après-midi, **ce** soir, **cette** nuit

## 3. The letters that sound different

> **RULE** **G** sounds like "zh" before e, i, y (girafe, garage) and like "g" elsewhere (gâteau). **J** is always "zh" (je, jour). **H** is silent (hôtel, homme). **R** comes from the throat (rue, rouge, Paris).

> **RULE** Final consonants are usually silent: Paris, vous, parlent. In a **liaison**, the silent final s turns into a "z" sound when the next word starts with a vowel or silent h.

| Written | Say it | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nous parlons | noo par-lon | p is a consonant: no liaison |
| nous achetons | noo-z-ach-ton | vowel next: liaison |
| vous avez | voo-z-avay | vowel next: liaison |
| ils ont | eel-z-on | vowel next: liaison |
| elles parlent | el parl | -ent of verbs is silent |

Vowels in French: a, e, i, o, u, y. A silent **h** behaves like a vowel (j'habite, l'hôtel).

## 4. The 5 accents

| Accent | On | What it does | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| aigu ´ | é only | "ay" sound, as in "day" | bébé, épice, parlé (spoke) vs parle (speak) |
| grave ` | è, à, ù | è = open "eh"; on à and ù it separates two words | père, frère, après; là (there) vs la (the); où (where) vs ou (or) |
| cédille ¸ | ç only | makes c sound like s before a, o, u | garçon, français, leçon, ça va |
| circonflexe ^ | â ê î ô û | often marks an old s; check the English cousin | hôpital (hospital), forêt (forest), fête (feast), île (isle) |
| tréma ¨ | ë ï ü | say the two vowels separately | Noël, maïs (corn) vs mais (but), naïf |

> **RULE** An accent can change the meaning: **où** = where, **ou** = or; **là** = there, **la** = the; **sûr** = sure, **sur** = on; **tâche** = task, **tache** = stain.

> **RULE** C before e or i already sounds like s (Francine, merci). It needs **ç** only before a, o, u: commençons, garçon, ça.

## 5. Subject pronouns

| French | English |
| --- | --- |
| je / j' | I (j' before a vowel or h) |
| tu | you (one person, informal) |
| il / elle / on | he / she / one, "we" in speech |
| nous | we |
| vous | you (formal, or more than one person) |
| ils | they (all boys, or a mixed group) |
| elles | they (all girls) |

> **WATCH OUT** One boy in a group of girls makes the whole group **ils**.

## Keep in mind

Easy slips to avoid:

- **mois** = month, **moins** = less: les **mois** de l'année.
- **l'après-midi** = the afternoon (midi alone = noon).
- **é** sounds like "ay" (écouter = ay-koo-tay), never "ee".
- **mangé** = eaten (past participle), not "ate".
- Welcoming someone? Say **Bienvenue !** (with the final e).
- False friends: une **âme** = a soul, un **état** = a state, un **vêtement** = a piece of clothing.
- **hôpital** always keeps its ô (the lost s from hospital).

8. Do you say the s in "vous avez"? In "vous parlez"?

10. What is the difference between **mais** and **maïs**?
